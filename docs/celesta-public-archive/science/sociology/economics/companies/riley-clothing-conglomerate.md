## History

Founded March 8, 2019 by 18-year-old Olivia Riley under the company name Riley Reactive Fabrics Company, this company started out fairly humbly in the form of Olivia's experiments with celestium as used for fabric, specifically in pursuit of a reactive "smartwear" that would end the need for manually custom-tailoring clothes for individuals with [CVCS](/celesta-public-archive/science/medical/cvcs.md) and [cryolasia](/celesta-public-archive/science/medical/cryolasia.md), by automatically adapting to their needs. Through R&D and testing with her siblings and family friends, her new technology quickly gained demand. By late 2019, she had scaled up and renamed the company to Riley Smartwear Corporation, having realized the unique properties of her reactive fabric meant she could create small samples which would automatically self-replicate to form complete outfits. However, she had not expected that the product would become so generally popular as a replacement for constantly having to buy new clothes to build out or update wardrobes, and found herself quickly scrambling to keep scaling up despite this celestium R&D innovation.

In November of 2019, Olivia realized she could, in theory, scale up further by buying out a smaller clothing manufacturer or general textiles company. In pursuit of this goal, she set up a waitlist for her product, promising returns within 6 months of signing up, and used the revenue inflow from preorders to save up for a purchase. The first merger was signed on January 16, 2020, between RSC and the also Celesta-headquartered Dovetail Designs Textiles, and mass production began on smartwear fabric a mere week later.

With the advent of the [Changewave](/celesta-public-archive/science/chemistry/celestium/changewave.md) phenomenon on [March 17, 2020](/celesta-public-archive/history/changewave-1.md), demand once again catapulted, and Olivia continued to scale the company up, making a number of increasingly scrutinized mergers and gobbling up textiles and clothing companies in the process, singlehandedly and unintentionally forming a monopoly in the process. In response to the unprecedented rise of the now Riley Clothing Conglomerate, the fashion and clothing markets began adapting by marketing their products as 'genuine luxury products' and sharply increasing the price tags to match, a phenomenon that has been ongoing since 2022 and has been dubbed 'denimonics' in political and economic circles.
